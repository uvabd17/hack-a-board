Brand guide.
How hackaboard looks, sounds, and treats people.

A short, opinionated brand book. Covers the wordmark, colour, type, component patterns, voice, and the rules we don't break — like never colouring a losing team in red.

Tokens below are the same CSS custom properties the app uses, so this doc is also the source of truth.

01Wordmark — the two forms

The brand has two forms. They are not interchangeable.

hack<a>board
visual logo · wordmark

Visual logo: the wordmark hack<a>board — literal angle brackets around a cyan-tinted a. The brackets are part of the brand identity, not decoration. Use this anywhere a logo is rendered: headers, hero, footer "powered by," wrapped slides, login pages, anywhere the mark is meant to act as a logo. Even if at small sizes the mark visually reads as "hackboard," the brackets stay.

hackaboard
prose form · spoken / written

Prose form: plain hackaboard, no brackets, no styling, all lowercase. Use this in body copy, page titles, meta descriptions, email subjects, legal copy, commit messages, marketing — anywhere the word appears as text rather than as a logo.

On a light background

hack<a>board
light-mode rendering

On light surfaces, the brackets shift to the darker cyan (--cyan-dim) so contrast holds. The black-text portion stays pure #0E0E12.

never

HackaBoard, Hackaboard, hackaBoard, hack-a-board, or any other casing. The brand is always lowercase, one word, no hyphen. Don't render the wordmark without the brackets, and don't render prose text with them.

the kindness rule

Red (--bad) is for errors and system failures only. It is never used for teams that didn't advance. Eliminated / cut teams are rendered in --text-mute with reduced opacity and gentle wording like "did not advance." Losing a hackathon stings on its own; the UI never piles on.

04Component patterns

The brutalist button

Border-2 on top + sides, border-4 on bottom — a hard offset shadow drawn in the border itself. Pressed state shrinks the bottom border to 2 and shifts the button down by 2px (active state, simulates physical click).

The screen chrome (mockups)

Three muted dots + a mono URL on a --bg-elev-2 bar above a --bg-elev body. Used in all product mockups so the reader knows they're looking at a screen, not a diagram.

05Voice & tone

How hackaboard sounds. Tight, declarative, no-fluff. Like a senior engineer who knows what they're talking about and respects your time.

principlewhat it means
terseShort sentences. Active voice. Cut every word that doesn't earn its place. "Pick a shape," not "Please select your preferred event shape."
declarativeState what is. Avoid hedging modals ("could," "might," "may want to") unless the uncertainty is real.
respectfulTreat organizers and participants as capable adults. No tutorialese, no "Don't worry!" reassurance.
specificNumbers over adjectives. "200 teams" beats "many teams." "Closes 17:00" beats "closing soon."
kind on losses"Did not advance," not "ELIMINATED." Grey, not red. The system never piles on.
lowercase brand"hackaboard," never "HackaBoard."

Pick a shape to start.
don't
Welcome! Let's get your awesome hackathon set up 🚀
do

Round 1 closed. 40 of 200 advance.
don't
Round 1 is OVER! 40 incredible teams have made it through to the next stage!!!
do

don't

06The hard rules — never do

rulewhy
Never "HackaBoard" / "Hackaboard"The brand is lowercase, one word, no exceptions. Logo form keeps the brackets; prose form drops them.
Never colour a losing team redRed = errors only. Eliminated teams are --text-mute + reduced opacity + "did not advance." Empathy is a brand value.
Never emojis in product UI copyAdds noise, dates the product, breaks the terse tone. Mockup illustrations may use icons (search, link); shipped UI strings don't.
Never exclamation marksThe system is calm and competent. Excitement is performative.
Never invent livenessIf nothing's happening on the board, show that nothing's happening. Don't fake motion to look engaging. Operational ticker (real info) is fine; fake metrics are not.
Never address an audienceThere isn't one. Build for the room — participants, judges, organizers. No livestream optics, no "Hello everyone watching!"
Never marketing words"Awesome," "amazing," "world-class," "powerful," "robust" — banned. State what the thing does, not how great it is.
Never hedge what you know"You may want to consider..." → "Add a judge." If the action is clear, say it.
