design choice · hidden until close

An online phase defaults to hidden — no live board during the five days. There's no audience and nothing to watch; a half-formed ranking creeping for days just creates anxiety. FullEvery team's rank and score numberMaximum transparency; teams can see exactly where they landed.
Ranks onlyPositions; score numbers hiddenAvoids "we lost by 0.3 marks" arguments and hides how close/far the field was.

Teams that don't advance are dimmed with "did not advance" — never red. Losing stings enough; the UI shouldn't pile on.

Does the next round start fresh, or add on?

That's the organizer's call — the scoreCarry toggle on the next phase:

fresh

The Grand Finale is judged standalone. Finale R1 scores got the 12 teams in — now everyone starts at zero. Innovate Bharat uses this.

cumulative

The Grand Finale score is added to the Finale R1 score. Final standing = the running total across both rounds.

Elimination and score-carry are independent toggles. A round can cut teams without carrying scores, or carry scores without cutting anyone.

Act 4 — referenceThe model behind itThe toggles, the leaderboard states, the roles, and why the judging design is the way it is.

ref 01The toggles that drive a judging phase

Every judging phase is defined by a small set of independent settings. The organizer sets them; the leaderboard, judge UI, and display all derive from them.

judgingStyle
rubric · shortlist · panel

What the judge actually does — score criteria, triage yes/no/maybe, or deliberate-and-announce.

timingMode
windowed · continuous

Judging open in a defined window, or open across the whole event with no sub-windows.

scoreCarry
fresh · cumulative

Does this round's score stand alone, or add to a running total across rounds.

advancement
none · top-N · threshold

Does the round cut teams — and by what rule. Independent of scoreCarry.

leaderboard
hidden · live

Visible during judging, or concealed until reveal. Smart default: online = hidden, offline = live. Organizer overrides.

assignment
open · per-judge

Any judge scores any team, or each judge gets a fixed subset / track.

No time bonus / time penalty — scoring rewards the idea, not the speed. No "judged by N judges" badge on the public board — that's noise to teams; judge-coverage lives in the organizer's view only. No audience voting, no spectator mode — there's no audience at tier 2/3 college hackathons.

ref 04Why these decisions — show the working

How the judging styles were chosen, what lost, and what's knowingly left out. Nothing here is a preference — it traces to research or an explicit constraint.

how these were decided

A research pass surveyed real judging practice — Devfolio's docs, the MLH guide, Unstop's organizer material, the SIH process, Devpost. Two independent design reviews (codex + gemini) stress-tested the architecture. Where the earlier draft disagreed with research, research won.

Seven mechanisms in the wild, filtered to three

Audience votingRejectedNo audience exists at tier 2/3 college hackathons. No crowd, no crowd vote.

Why shortlist is its own format — and why it's online

Rubric — evaluate

Time per team. Rate criteria 1–5. A multi-criteria form. Works at 20–60 teams, in a hall.

Shortlist — triage

200 teams, judges reviewing decks remotely over days. One fast call: advance, maybe, no. One tap.

Research confirmed Indian college hackathon Round 1 is overwhelmingly an online deck/PPT screening — Codenovate '25, Breaking Enigma (VIT Pune, "top 50 shortlisted"), Odoo × Allenhouse, IIIT Naya Raipur. Different judge task, different place, different window length — so it's a distinct format, not a flag on rubric.

If all judges submitted independently, the system would average individual opinions — which is rubric scoring, and discards the consensus the deliberation produced. Independent scoring is still available: the organizer just picks rubric for that phase. Panel is the choice for announce-only finales.

ref 05Setup ease — how organizers actually use it

The toggles are clean for engineers and murder for a first-time organizer. The fix is layered UX: templates do the work for 80% of cases, smart defaults handle the next 15%, and the toggles only appear when someone drills in. Organizers never see schema words like scoreCarry.

A first-time organizer creates a working hackathon in under 3 minutes — pick a template, fill name + dates, add judges, hit publish. An organizer who needs custom behaviour drills in. Power and approachability without trading one for the other.

ref 07Build path — leveraging what exists

The codebase already has most of the data model and most of the runtime. The build is mostly rename / extend / refactor — not rewrite.

What we reuse

Data model

Round (becomes Phase), Criterion, Score, Judge, JudgingAttempt, Submission, Team, Participant, Hackathon. All survive. New optional fields added.

Runtime

Leaderboard Redis cache, socket emit pipeline, QR auth route (generalizes to magic-link auth), rate-limit infra, access-control gates, dashboard / manage shell.

Scoring formula

The current formula (judge avg × round weight) becomes the RubricScorer strategy — extracted into its own module, unchanged math. Zero behaviour change on existing hackathons.

UI primitives

BrandMark / BrandFooter, the brutalist card pattern, dark-theme tokens, scoring form, judge dashboard shell, dev test-organizer login.

What we add

LayerNew
SchemaOn Phase: judgingStyle, timingMode, scoreCarry, advancement, leaderboardVisibility, assignmentMode, phaseType (judging / non-judging). New tables: ShortlistVote, PanelDecision. All additions nullable / defaulted — existing rows valid as-is.
Scorer registryRubricScorer (extracted), ShortlistScorer (new), PanelScorer (new). Common RoundScorer interface.
UITemplate picker, phase setup wizard, plain-language config form, advanced-settings disclosure, pre-flight checklist, hidden/reveal display states, judge review queue (shortlist), captain drag-order (panel), organizer progress monitor.
Routes/h/[slug]/judge/[token] magic-link entry, /manage/phases, /manage/phases/[id]/config, /manage/publish (pre-flight).

What we deliberately retire

Hackathon.timeBonusRate, timePenaltyRate, Submission.timeBonus — kept on existing hackathons for history, never used by new ones. Time scoring is gone.

The build sequence

step 1~3 daysSchema migration. Add nullable fields with safe defaults. Rename Round→Phase at the API surface (DB column can stay). Existing hackathons run unchanged.zero-impact
step 2~3 daysRubricScorer refactor. Extract the current formula into a strategy module. Wire the leaderboard data path through the registry. Pure refactor — behaviour identical.verify with old hackathons
step 3~5 daysSetup wizard + templates. Template picker, plain-language phase config, progressive disclosure, pre-flight checklist. Additive UI, no judging-behaviour change.organizer-facing
step 4~4 daysHidden / reveal leaderboard states. Display gains a state machine. Smart defaults per phase (online=hidden, offline=live). Reveal trigger + animation.low risk
step 5~7 daysShortlist style. ShortlistVote + ShortlistScorer + judge review queue UI + magic-link generic auth (generalize from QR route) + organizer progress monitor.first new format
step 6~5 daysPanel deliberation. PanelDecision + PanelScorer + captain drag-order UI + reveal animation. Finals support.unblocks finals

Total: ~30 working days (~6 weeks). Each step ships independently — we can stop after step 4 with a working "rubric only with hidden/reveal" product, or after step 5 with rubric + shortlist. Nothing requires step 7 to be functional.

migration philosophy

Every existing hackathon must keep working with zero data migration on day 1. New behaviour rides on new optional fields; old hackathons see no UI change until they're explicitly opted in. We don't rewrite the runtime — we extend it.

ref 09tl;dr

A hackathon is an ordered list of phases. Each judging phase carries its own toggles — judgingStyle, timingMode, scoreCarry, advancement, leaderboard visibility — and the leaderboard, judge UI, and hall display all derive from them. Round 1 is an online shortlist (remote review queue, hidden board, reveal at close). The finale is in-hall rubric scoring (hidden during judging, organizer-triggered reveal, gentle grey for teams that don't advance) and ends in a panel-deliberated grand finale. CSV import ships first to onboard organizers; the judging redesign follows. No time scoring, no audience, no spectators.
